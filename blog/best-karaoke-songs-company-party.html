50 Best Karaoke Songs for a Company Party (That Won't Get HR Involved)

By Daniel Lopez · PopUp Karaoke · NW Indiana & Chicagoland · January 2026 · 10 min read

Company karaoke is one of the best team-building activities money can buy — and one of the easiest to accidentally make awkward. The wrong song in front of your VP or CFO can be a story that follows you for years. This list takes the guesswork out of it. These 50 songs are crowd-pleasing, office-safe, and will have your coworkers cheering instead of cringing.

Why Company Karaoke Works (When It's Done Right)

Research on workplace bonding consistently shows that shared vulnerability creates the strongest team connections. Karaoke is organized, joyful vulnerability — you stand up in front of your colleagues, take a risk, and they cheer for you regardless of your talent level. That shared experience rewires how people perceive each other at work. The colleague you thought was cold or difficult becomes the person who belted out "Total Eclipse of the Heart" with zero shame, and suddenly they're human again.

The key is removing the barriers. If people are scared to pick up the mic because they might choose something inappropriate, they won't go up. But if the song list is pre-curated for the workplace context, participation skyrockets. That's what this list is for.

The Golden Rules of Workplace Karaoke

  • No explicit lyrics. Even clean versions of songs with known explicit lyrics can feel awkward in a professional context. When in doubt, skip it.
  • No songs with romantic or sexual themes directed at colleagues. "Sexual Healing" is a great karaoke song. Not at the company party.
  • Nothing politically charged. This isn't the night for protest anthems or anything that could be interpreted as a statement about company policy.
  • Big choruses win. Songs where the entire room can join in — even from their seats — are always better than technically impressive solo showcases.
  • Familiarity beats quality. A song everyone knows sung badly is more fun than a song only two people know sung perfectly.
  • Duets and group songs flatten hierarchy. When the CEO and the intern are both on stage together, magic happens.

Top 10 Crowd-Pleasers Everyone Knows

Start with these. They work in every room, every time.

  1. "Don't Stop Believin'" — Journey · The universal karaoke opener. Every generation knows it.
  2. "Sweet Caroline" — Neil Diamond · The ba-ba-ba crowd response makes everyone feel like part of the act.
  3. "Bohemian Rhapsody" — Queen · Six people can divide the parts. The room goes insane at the guitar solo.
  4. "Africa" — Toto · Genuinely everyone knows this one now. Huge singalong potential.
  5. "Living on a Prayer" — Bon Jovi · Built for crowds. Works at 9pm with strangers. Works at midnight with coworkers.
  6. "Piano Man" — Billy Joel · The closing slow build everyone loves. Also appropriate at any company event whatsoever.
  7. "Mr. Brightside" — The Killers · Universally beloved. The intro alone gets people off their chairs.
  8. "Total Eclipse of the Heart" — Bonnie Tyler · Theatrical. Ridiculous. Perfect. The key change alone is worth it.
  9. "Roxanne" — The Police · The "Roxanne!" drinking game has made this famous for a reason. Clean and appropriate.
  10. "Build Me Up Buttercup" — The Foundations · The crowd call-and-response format means nobody needs to know the verses.

Top 10 Classic Rock Anthems

These draw out the coworkers who swear they "never sing" — until the first guitar chord hits.

  1. "Sweet Home Alabama" — Lynyrd Skynyrd · Cross-generational crowd-pleaser. The opening riff does the work.
  2. "More Than a Feeling" — Boston · Perfect for the office rock purist who needs an excuse to get up.
  3. "Highway to Hell" — AC/DC · Clean, iconic, and the "Hey-yay" chorus draws everyone in.
  4. "Born to Run" — Bruce Springsteen · Long but worth it. Crowd singalong potential is elite.
  5. "We Will Rock You / We Are the Champions" — Queen · The stomp-stomp-clap intro gets the whole room involved before anyone sings a note.
  6. "Eye of the Tiger" — Survivor · Irresistible. Especially with air guitar accompaniment from coworkers.
  7. "Pour Some Sugar on Me" — Def Leppard · The workplace version is the clean version — and it still works completely.
  8. "The Final Countdown" — Europe · The intro keyboard riff alone launches the crowd. Best used as the night's penultimate song.
  9. "Carry On Wayward Son" — Kansas · For the office prog-rock nerd who's been waiting for this moment their entire career.
  10. "Free Fallin'" — Tom Petty · Slower and more singable than most rock tracks. Works for the person who wants to perform but isn't ready to shout.

Top 10 Pop Hits (2010s–2020s)

For the colleagues who only listen to Spotify and haven't touched classic rock since high school.

  1. "Rolling in the Deep" — Adele · The karaoke standard of the 2010s. Every room has at least one person who's been waiting to sing this.
  2. "Uptown Funk" — Mark Ronson ft. Bruno Mars · Gets everyone moving. Clean, fun, universally loved.
  3. "Shake It Off" — Taylor Swift · Self-explanatory. Yes, your CFO secretly knows every word.
  4. "Happy" — Pharrell Williams · Impossible not to smile during. Sets the right energy early in the night.
  5. "Can't Stop the Feeling" — Justin Timberlake · Clean, dance-y, universally recognizable. Everyone sings along from their seats.
  6. "Shallow" — Lady Gaga & Bradley Cooper · The duet of the decade. Works with two willing coworkers and makes for great video content.
  7. "Havana" — Camila Cabello · The "Havana, na-na-na" is a crowd singalong waiting to happen.
  8. "Someone Like You" — Adele · The office power ballad. Someone will nail the bridge and the room will be stunned.
  9. "Old Town Road" — Lil Nas X · Clean, fun, easy to sing, works across age groups. The office Gen Z contingent will love you for it.
  10. "Blinding Lights" — The Weeknd · The 80s-flavored synth pop is oddly comforting and everyone knows the chorus.

Top 10 Throwbacks: 80s & 90s

Nostalgia is the most powerful force in a karaoke room. These songs get the 40+ crowd off their chairs.

  1. "Girls Just Wanna Have Fun" — Cyndi Lauper · Universally appropriate. Works as a solo or group performance.
  2. "Don't You (Forget About Me)" — Simple Minds · The Breakfast Club ending. The fist-pump at the end is mandatory.
  3. "Take On Me" — a-ha · The falsetto challenge draws brave volunteers. The rest of the room cringes and cheers simultaneously.
  4. "Footloose" — Kenny Loggins · Instant energy. Especially after someone's had their second drink.
  5. "I Want It That Way" — Backstreet Boys · 90s nostalgia perfection. Crowd knows every word without thinking about it.
  6. "Wannabe" — Spice Girls · Group performance. Requires exactly five people and zero talent. Perfect for a team.
  7. "Ice Ice Baby" — Vanilla Ice · The ironic pick that always lands. Especially if the CEO does it.
  8. "Baby Got Back" — Sir Mix-a-Lot · Technically workplace-safe in the clean version. Generates the most laughs per minute of any song on this list. Use judgment on the room.
  9. "Smells Like Teen Spirit" — Nirvana · The person who picks this either absolutely nails it or absolutely doesn't. Either way the room goes wild.
  10. "MMMBop" — Hanson · The ultimate ironic throwback. Nobody actually knows the verses. Everyone sings the chorus.

10 Bonus Picks for the Brave Souls

These require confidence and/or a couple drinks. They pay off enormously when they land.

  1. "My Heart Will Go On" — Celine Dion · The power ballad to end all power ballads. High risk, high reward.
  2. "I Will Always Love You" — Whitney Houston · The ultimate vocal challenge. The room holds its breath.
  3. "Bohemian Rhapsody" — Queen (solo version) · Brave. Truly brave. Deserves a standing ovation regardless of outcome.
  4. "Man in the Mirror" — Michael Jackson · Earnest. Emotional. Works better than you'd expect at an office party.
  5. "Nessun Dorma" — Puccini · If you have a trained vocalist on the team, this is the moment. The rest of the room will genuinely not know what to do.
  6. "Bohemian Like You" — Dandy Warhols · For the office indie kid who wants something unexpected and cool.
  7. "Gold Digger" — Kanye West (clean) · The clean version still works as a performance. HR cleared, crowd approved.
  8. "Baby" — Justin Bieber · Ironically or earnestly — both work. Either way the crowd erupts.
  9. "All By Myself" — Celine Dion · Best deployed mid-party as a satirical cry for help. Works if you commit to the bit.
  10. "We Found Love" — Rihanna (karaoke version) · The final 90 seconds turn the whole room into a rave. End the night with it.

Songs to Avoid at Work Events (A Friendly PSA)

These are songs that seem fine until you're performing them in front of your manager:

  • Anything with explicit lyrics you can't un-say in a professional context — even if you think you know the clean edit, one slip is enough
  • "9 to 5" by Dolly Parton — technically fine, but do you really want to perform a song about hating your boss at the company party?
  • Songs about infidelity or breakups that could be misread as directed at a colleague
  • Extremely long songs with no crowd singalong potential — nobody wants to watch a 7-minute solo performance
  • Songs where the performance requires physical contact with other performers

How to Structure the Night for Maximum Participation

The difference between a company karaoke night where three brave people sing for an hour and one where 80% of attendees get up is structure. Here's how to engineer participation:

  • Open with someone in leadership going first. When the VP or department head goes up before anyone else, it signals that nobody is too important to be silly. Participation follows.
  • Run a department challenge early. "Marketing vs. Sales" duet battle gets teams involved as units, which reduces individual pressure.
  • Use a rotating queue with friendly pressure. Have the host call on tables for suggestions rather than waiting for volunteers. Most people say yes when asked directly.
  • Build to the group anthem at the end. Save your biggest crowd singalong for the final 15 minutes. Everyone on their feet, everyone singing. That's the moment they'll talk about Monday morning.

Icebreaker Songs to Open the Night

Use these in the first 30 minutes while people are still arriving and getting comfortable:

  • "Happy" — Pharrell Williams (instantly positive, zero intimidation)
  • "Can't Stop the Feeling" — Justin Timberlake (everyone knows it, very singable)
  • "Uptown Funk" — Bruno Mars (gets people moving without requiring stage commitment)
  • "Build Me Up Buttercup" — The Foundations (audience call-and-response from their seats)
  • "Sweet Caroline" — Neil Diamond (the ba-ba-ba moment gets the whole room participating without anyone going on stage)
