Holiday Office Party Karaoke: The Complete Planning Guide

By Daniel Lopez · PopUp Karaoke · NW Indiana & Chicagoland · October 2026 · 9 min read

The holiday party is the one night a year when the whole company is in the same room just to have fun. Karaoke turns that night into something people actually remember, as long as it's planned right. Here's how to book it, run it, and fill the queue, plus the songs that work every time.

Why Book Your Holiday Party Entertainment Now

Most companies start looking for December entertainment after Thanksgiving. By then, the most popular party nights are often already taken. Friday and Saturday nights in the first three weeks of December are the most requested dates of the entire year for party entertainment, and venues, caterers, and hosts all fill up on the same nights.

If your team is planning a holiday party, October and early November are the right time to lock in the date. Booking early also gives you options: you can choose your preferred night instead of taking whatever is left.

Pick a Date That Gets People to Show Up

  • Early December weeknights (Tuesday through Thursday) are easier to book and often see better attendance than a Saturday, when people have family plans.
  • Friday after work is the classic choice. Start early so people with kids can still come.
  • A January "after the holidays" party is becoming more popular. Nobody's calendar is full, and it gives the team something to look forward to after the rush.
  • Lunch or afternoon events work for teams with shift schedules or remote staff who'll only come in for the day.

Plan the Room Before You Plan the Playlist

A great karaoke night depends on the space more than people expect. Before you book, check a few basics with your venue:

  • Floor space for a performance area. It doesn't need a stage, just a clear spot in front of the crowd where everyone can see the singer and the lyrics screen.
  • Standard power outlets near the performance area.
  • Sound limits. Some restaurants and banquet halls have volume rules or neighbors. A professional system can be tuned to the room.
  • Load-in access and setup time. Ask when the room opens for vendors so the system is ready before guests arrive.
  • Insurance requirements. Many corporate venues ask entertainment vendors for a certificate of insurance. PopUp Karaoke can provide one.

A Night That Builds: How to Structure the Party

The holiday parties people remember have a shape. Here's the flow we use for corporate events:

  • Arrival and dinner: background music at conversation volume. Let people settle in.
  • Warm-up: the host opens with a crowd-friendly song and sets the tone: this is for fun, nobody is being judged.
  • Leadership goes first: when a manager or executive takes the first turn, everyone else relaxes. It's the single most effective way to get participation.
  • Team rounds: department versus department, or table versus table. Group songs take the pressure off individuals.
  • Open queue: now the volunteers come on their own. The host keeps the list moving and mixes slow songs with high-energy ones.
  • The finale: one big song the whole room sings together. That's the moment people talk about at work on Monday.

Holiday Party Games That Get Everyone Involved

  • Ugly Sweater Sing-Off: anyone in an ugly sweater gets priority in the queue. The ugliest sweater picks the final song.
  • Department Battle: each team picks one song and performs it together. The crowd votes with applause.
  • Name That Holiday Tune: the host plays the first few seconds of a song, and the first table to name it wins.
  • Duet Draw: pull two names from a hat and hand them a duet. It mixes people who don't usually work together.
  • Boss's Choice: the team picks the song that leadership has to sing. Always a highlight.

Winter Songs for a Mixed Crowd

Not everyone on your team celebrates Christmas. Mixing in seasonal winter songs keeps the party festive and welcoming for everyone, and a good host balances the playlist automatically.

  1. "Let It Snow! Let It Snow! Let It Snow!" — Dean Martin · Winter, not Christmas, so it works for everyone in the room.
  2. "Winter Wonderland" — Darlene Love · Seasonal and inclusive with a big, bright chorus.
  3. "Let It Go" — Frozen (2013 film) · The song every team has someone secretly waiting to belt.

8 Year-Round Crowd-Pleasers to Close the Night

  2. "Don't Stop Believin'" — Journey · The universal karaoke anthem.
  3. "September" — Earth, Wind & Fire · Gets people dancing even if they never touch the mic.
  4. "Livin' on a Prayer" — Bon Jovi · Save the key change for the end of the night.

  8. "Shut Up and Dance" — Walk The Moon · A modern singalong that brings the energy back up.

  • "Baby, It's Cold Outside." It splits rooms. At a work party, there are plenty of other duets that don't start a debate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should we book karaoke for a holiday party?

Book in October or early November if you can. Friday and Saturday nights in the first three weeks of December are the most requested dates of the year, so they fill first.

Will our team actually sing?

Yes, with the right structure. Having a leader go first, starting with group songs, and a host who keeps things light are what turn a quiet room into a full queue.

Can you keep the song list work-appropriate?

Yes. Let us know when you book and your host will steer the queue toward clean, office-friendly picks.

Do we need a stage?

No. A clear area in front of the crowd and standard power outlets are all we need. We bring the sound system, microphones, lighting, and lyrics display.
